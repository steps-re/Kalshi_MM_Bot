### 1. Exit Model Treats Passive Resting Fill as Guaranteed (Overstates PnL by ~0.5–1.0¢/trade)
* **Target:** Claim C2 ("Exit convention error worth +0.671c/trade") & Method Notes (`exit_touch = ask at horizon for buy`).
* **Mechanism:** Marking exit at the touch assumes a resting limit order fills with 100% probability at $t+h$ without crossing the spread. In reality, a resting limit order at the touch suffers from **adverse selection**: it fills only when the market trades through it (adverse price move) and remains unfilled when the market moves in your favor.
* **Bias:** Strongly **positive / optimistic**. It credits half-to-full spread capture on trades that would never execute passively in live trading.
* **Test:** Compute PnL under immediate taker exit (cross the spread at $t+h$: sell at `bid`, buy at `ask`). If expectancy drops below zero or fees, the edge is purely an artifact of phantom passive fills.

### 2. Microstructure Bid-Ask Bounce / Mechanical Control Confound (Manufactures the Dose-Response)
* **Target:** Claim C4 ("Dose-response with a no-signal control rules out drift/decay").
* **Mechanism:** 
  $$\text{OBI} = \frac{\text{bid\_sz} - \text{ask\_sz}}{\text{bid\_sz} + \text{ask\_sz}}$$
  When a taker trade hits the ask, the ask depletes, mechanical imbalance immediately spikes to $\text{OBI} > 0.9$, and the mid-price is elevated. Marking forward returns from this state measures post-trade mean reversion and standard bid-ask bounce, not predictive order-flow alpha. The control $|\text{OBI}| < 0.2$ occurs when both sides are wide/untouched, making negative returns the baseline due to spread/fees.
* **Bias:** Strongly **positive** for extreme bands relative to control; manufactures artificial monotonicity.
* **Test:** Condition OBI calculation on samples where **no trade occurred** in the prior 1.0s, or measure price changes from $t + \delta$ (e.g., $100\text{ms}$ latency buffer) rather than the instantaneous arrival tick.

### 3. Queue Priority & Capacity Fallacy on the Thin Side
* **Target:** Claim C6, C7 ("Live fills land on displayed touch; 74 contracts available").
* **Mechanism:** Extreme OBI ($>0.9$) means the side you must cross (`crossable`) is near depletion ($1-10$ contracts). At 25 contracts per trade, your order walks the book, eating depth into worse price levels, or you arrive after another taker has wiped the touch (fill failure / race condition).
* **Bias:** Strongly **positive**. Assumes infinite top-of-book liquidity at the displayed touch.
* **Test:** Evaluate markouts strictly requiring `crossable >= target_contracts` (e.g., $\ge 25$) and simulate book walking across price levels using full order book depth instead of top-of-book touch.

### 4. Asymmetric Horizon Censoring Drops Large Moves
* **Target:** `extract()` lines:
  ```python
  if offset + MAX_HORIZON > series_end:
      censored += 1
      continue
  ```
* **Mechanism:** In 15M/hourly binary contracts, order-book recording sessions frequently terminate upon settlement or early market suspension when a contract hits $0$ or $100$. Triggers fired in the final 30 seconds before a binary resolution are systematically discarded if the stream cuts at settlement.
* **Bias:** **Biased / Uncontrolled** depending on direction, removing high-volatility termination events.
* **Test:** Check the distribution of `to_close` on censored records. If censored entries cluster near expiration, rerun with variable horizon truncated at `min(horizon, to_close)`.

### 5. Data Dredging on KXBTCD 2-5¢ Sub-slice (Overfitting)
* **Target:** Claim C5 ("KXBTCD, entry price 2-5c long-equivalent, last 15 min before close").
* **Mechanism:** This slice is a 4-way conditional subset: venue (`KXBTCD`) $\times$ deep OTM/ITM price (`2-5c`) $\times$ expiry window (`last 15m`) $\times$ signal strength (`obi > .9`). With 24–95 markets, this is classic multi-hypothesis dredging. When tested on the identical 15M contract family, the signal collapses (+0.2¢, negative net).
* **Bias:** Heavily **positive / overfit**.
* **Test:** Apply standard Bonferroni or White's Reality Check across the Cartesian product of all tested slices (price bands $\times$ time-to-close windows $\times$ venues). The $z=1.15$ difference between sets confirms low statistical power, not true replication.
